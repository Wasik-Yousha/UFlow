Sound

Eight cues, synthesised at launch rather than shipped as audio files — a short noise burst through a band-pass with a fast decay is exactly what a latching plastic key sounds like. Master level 0.35.
.recordDown
Heavy latching key going down. The deepest cue in the set.
.stopDown
Shorter, harder clunk — the key releasing the latch.
.keyUp
Soft release tick under every key coming back up.
.tabSwitch
Small detent tick, like a rotary selector passing a stop.
.copy
Light click for Copy on a transcript row.
.add
Click with a short rising tail — a dictionary entry landing.
.delete
Dull thunk, no tail.
.error
Low buzz for a rejected or unsafe dictionary entry.

Everything else I will build straight from the references.

1 · The left nameplate

Your references say SONY / CFS-715S / FM/SW/MW / STEREO CASSETTE-CORDER. That is someone else's trademark on your app.

My call: drop in your metal UFlow_textlogo.png plate where SONY sits — it is already the right shape and finish — and set the model text to UF-715S / ON-DEVICE / SPEECH TRANSCRIBER. Same layout, same rhythm, your brand.

2 · The right-hand plate

Light reference shows the UFlow logo mark there. Dark reference shows a Bangladesh rickshaw plate instead.

My call: the logo mark in both, so the app looks like one product. Say the word if the Bangladesh plate is deliberate and you want it kept in dark.

3 · Dictionary row dots

Light reference cycles the dots through four brand colours. Dark reference makes them all green.

My call: the four-colour cycle in both — it is already in the tokens as Tok.Brand.cycle and it reuses colours the app already owns, where the green would be a fifth colour that appears nowhere else.
